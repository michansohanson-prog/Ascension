_Ribbit._

The sound splintered her dreams as Petra felt a sudden thump on her chest. With a dramatic startle, she sat upright in bed, causing the cricket to tumble back into the blankets.
“Oh no, little one, I am so sorry,” the red dragonborn’s voice was gravelly as she attempted to free the winged frog from where it had thrashed itself to tangles in her clothing, croaking its discontent. The joints in her fingers sounded like rocks grinding together as she moved, the noise disconcerting enough to give the cricket pause—Petra Helm took advantage of the moment to loose the colorful creature’s claw from her lace nightgown.
Free at last, it hopped from her duvet to the wooden surface of her desk, its large black eyes glaring. Its mouth fell agape, long pink tongue unfurling to reveal a pearlescent bead.
“Petra Helm,” The voice was unfamiliar, imperious and instructive, radiating out from the magic implanted in the pearl. “You must come to the capitol building. You must come now.”

Mere moments later, the buildings of [Enziret](Locations/Locations.md#Enziret) were flying by her in the night, illuminated by the moon and the odd forgotten lantern. In the 346 years of her own [Ascendancy](Ascendency/Ascendency.md), Petra had sponsored many of the architects herself—she remembered the proud face of a genasi man as he had placed the last multicolored tile into the whorl of rainbow decorating the wall of his home, and the deliberate precision it had required to raise the spire of the coiled temple a few streets down.
Above them all, the great monolith of [Enzi](Gods/Gods.md#Enzi) presided silently.
It was his gifts that helped Petra move so quickly through the streets of her kingdom, as the terrain and herself were one and the same. She was sand, stone, and arth; and where she had passed, there was no evidence that she had been there at all. Overhead, Enzi was a dark eclipse of the night sky, incomprehensibly large—the buildings were built up to and around him, the capitol building before his breast, underneath his bowed head. Large as the palace was, it was dwarfed a hundred times over by its God.
It would have been understandable to stop to admire the elaborate stone building, its intricate colored carvings representing the work of thousands of talented artisans, but as Petra’s body shifted from sand into red dragon scales her golden eyes remained pointed skywards as if they could meet the gaze of her sleeping deity.
The leviathan did not open his eyes. He never had, not for anyone alive younger than a God.
Nevertheless, she always looked. Still sleeping, she thought, making her way to the palace doors. The ornate pathway was accented by lit torches, the flames casting silhouettes on the carved motifs of Enzi’s ascension that decorated the stones. The guardian sentries had already opened the doors when Petra reached them, and the large frame of an orc stood with crossed arms, her long hair braided down to her knees.
“Thank Enzi you came.”
“You needn’t have waited for me, Etienne,” Petra chided, clasping her friend on the shoulder. Whatever smile Petra had been summoning died when she saw the look that the Constable returned. It was then she could hear the faint sounds of shouting, from down the hall…
“I’m sorry, Ascendant. I’ll fill you in as much as I can while we walk.”

“I demand a modicum of fucking dignity in this room!” The magically enhanced voice of the Magistrate overpowered the ministerial chambers, and Petra allowed a moment of sympathy for the swollen crowd of politicians, dignitaries, merchants, and who knows who else who had managed to get an invitation to the meeting. Normally composed, the sight of Dagda Knox’s seething countenance caused the assembly to fall back from where they’d been pressing forward. The tabaxi woman’s fangs glittered with spittle as the flat-faced politician continued to ream the ill-behaved congregation, her nails gouging deep into the wood of the podium. Petra knew she should be intervening, that it was her responsibility to restore order, but her mind was still mulling over what Etienne had told her: someone had finally been arrested for the Great Fire. Instinctively, her hand wandered to her own flank, where the burn had never properly healed.
Even as an Ascendant. Touching it now still caused the pain to flare.
He had been so powerful, even right under Enzi’s influence.
So many people had died… more than under any Ascendant’s watch ever. The guilt settled heavily into her gut, and she looked up again to the citizens of her home. They were emboldened again, shouting back at Dagda—some scarred like Petra, some holding mementos of the dead. Etienne’s guards were flanking the crowd now, and Etienne had a firmer grip on her staff than Petra would have liked to see when up against their own people. How many were there? It seemed the entire population of Enziret was attempting to crush into the capitol building. So many, and at such a late hour… They pulsated around the raised podium in a mass of rage. “He killed my daughter, let me see him!” She knew the man who yelled, had been a patron of his bakery. He was nearly unrecognizable now for the hate in his eyes. “He must pay! We deserve justice!” The genasi woman had once been known for her beauty, but the majority of her skin now was dark, contorting burn scars. Petra knew how much pain they must cause her. A never ending tide of animosity roiled around them. Reflexively, the Ascendant felt her core reach for the comfort of Enzi’s Tranquility—but it was overwhelmed by the ire vomited from every aggrieved Enziretan who had come for their revenge.
Recognition of something dark trickled into her mind like ice water. Wherever her gaze fell, she knew their story—she recognized them all. The Sanctuary’s own Eminence, Sadivir, stood quietly weeping in the throngs. He had lost both his husband and child to the Fire. Tesni Annick, the sweet Master of the Palace Stables, who had never forgiven herself for every animal she’d been unable to evacuate… Her face was beastly, her teeth gnashing as she pressed her body closer to the podium. Each legislator, dignitary… hundreds of people affected by loss—they were all represented, every family of Enziret. Of course they would consider it vital, to have their voices heard on such an occasion, but all of them? And so many, at this hour?
“ETIENNE!” The volume, the shock of her voice silenced even Dagda, who was at that moment chiding Enziret’s leading importer of silks. Petra found herself uncertain, but she could not stop the knot in her gut. “Etienne! Who… who called this meeting?”
The orc furrowed her brow, looking at Petra for a moment, before her gaze went back to Dagda. Petra looked too, to see the Magistrate aghast, suddenly trembling as she shook her head no. Petra was already moving towards her, preternaturally fast.
“Magistrate, we need to leave. I think someone wanted everyone here, —” Petra never finished the sentence. What happened next was over before a second could come to pass.
For most, the rupture of the world and the end of life was simultaneous. The part of Petra that could still see was envious of them, to be gone in that blink. It was agonizingly slow—soundless, floating. She saw the molecules that composed everything and everyone she had ever known be torn asunder, tossed together chaotically and skywards in a plume of black. As she watched the fabric of her hands unwind, a curiosity overtook the stark horror.
Each fragment of her flesh came undone in spools of golden light, forming a taut thread pointed towards the sun. Petra unraveled, the remains of her body spinning up, up, up. The threads had woven together, becoming a cord, and at last Petra understood, just as the last of her began to uncoil. It took every bit of strength she still had left as as Ascendant to turn what remained of her eyes to see, but she saw them, just as the last bit of her unfurled back into pure magic.
Pools of golden fire, brighter than the sun, blinked once in the darkness.
Enzi had opened his eyes.
